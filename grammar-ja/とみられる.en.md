# とみられる

## Meaning
A clause followed by **とみられる / と見られる** can present an assessment: something is considered or expected to be the case. News reporting often uses it to describe a conclusion drawn from the available information. It does not identify who made the assessment or establish that the reported proposition is true.

The supported construction requires a nominal assessment target or a plain finite clause ending before quotative **と**, followed by **み / 見 + られる**. It includes plain, polite, past, negative assessment, polite past, polite negative, negative past and polite negative past forms. A negative inside the reported clause, as in **再開しないとみられる**, differs from negating the assessment itself, as in **故障だとみられない**. The ending component exposes the latter negation when present.

Selections cover the quotative marker, assessment predicate, suffix and supported inflected endings. Nominal/clause attachment is evidence, not a claim to identify the complete reported clause. Literal **鳥を見られる**, trial **読んでみる**, and stitching across sentence/quotation boundaries are excluded. Aspect chains and quotation-ending attachments are outside this finite rule. Confidence is unassessed; sentence meaning still requires context.

The comma-continuation form **とみられ、…** covers an assessment joined to the next clause. Its selection ends at **られ**; the comma is required scope evidence. A bare **とみられ** ending, or one followed by a full stop, remains outside this form's coverage.

The short polite-negative form **みられません** requires following punctuation or a supported connective/final particle. Its selection ends at **ません**, while the polite-negative-past selection includes **でした**. An input ending immediately after **ません** is currently outside this short form's coverage; add sentence-ending punctuation to analyze it.
